## 1. The main idea

Build the smallest useful business operation first. Prove that it can produce real, trackable results before expanding the software, hiring more agents, paying for more infrastructure, or building major MMORPG features.

The growth loop is:

**Small business pilot → measured results → verified net income → protected reserves → CEO-approved small upgrade → test → repeat.**

The AI Director researches, measures, estimates, and recommends. The CEO makes every funding and scope approval. No fixed reinvestment percentage or revenue target is assumed in advance.

The long-term vision remains BOTH pillars:
- AI affiliate/content business as the first revenue priority.
- Cyborg Cheebee Mega World / MMORPG as the long-term product and community experience.

The business must not depend on the game being online. The game must not be allowed to consume unapproved business funds.

## 2. Rules that govern every phase

1. **Earn before expanding:** prioritize a working, low-cost business workflow before major game development.
2. **Use actual numbers:** separate gross sales/commissions, pending amounts, confirmed amounts, reversals, expenses, and net results.
3. **No guaranteed income:** affiliate or content revenue is uncertain; never treat projections as actual income.
4. **CEO approval required:** AI may recommend but cannot approve budgets, move funds, buy services, or deploy a funded upgrade.
5. **One small batch at a time:** define scope, maximum budget, success criteria, tests, and rollback before work starts.
6. **Re-approval on overrun:** pause if cost or scope materially exceeds the CEO-approved limit.
7. **Protect Phase D v8:** preserve the baseline and private data; add new features as isolated modules behind feature flags.
8. **Do not automate around platform rules:** use official, authorized APIs/integrations. If unsupported, prepare drafts for manual publishing.
9. **Separate ledgers:** business finance, affiliate commissions, Bazaar orders, development budget, player reward budget, Game Coins, XP, and Company Reward Credits must not be mixed.
10. **No premature player earning promises:** player rewards are game-only, require separate approved funding and eligibility, and are not cash, wages, or guaranteed income.
11. **Security and legal obligations first:** account security, rights, disclosures, taxes/fees, refunds, platform terms, backups, and privacy are not optional upgrades.
12. **A plan is not an implementation:** mark an item DONE only after code, tests, installation instructions, and acceptance checks pass.

## 3. Start with the smallest business pilot

### Pilot 0 — Manual-assisted content and affiliate workflow

**Purpose:** validate the business process at the lowest practical cost before building a large automation platform.

**Initial scope:**
- One approved content channel to start.
- One carefully selected product/topic category.
- A small, CEO-approved list of products or content topics.
- AI-assisted research, script/caption drafting, and quality checks.
- Human review before publishing.
- Official affiliate links and required disclosures only when the user's affiliate account and program terms permit them.
- A simple tracking sheet/dashboard for content published, views, clicks, orders/conversions where reported, confirmed commission, costs, and net result.

**Do not build yet:** multi-platform auto-publishing, a large agent fleet, paid cloud scaling, a complex analytics warehouse, player reward distribution, multiplayer, or MMORPG expansion.

**Pilot workflow:**
1. CEO confirms the content channel, topic/product category, and allowed work budget.
2. Research captures source, date checked, product facts, confidence, and permitted use.
3. AI drafts one content item using only supported claims.
4. Quality review checks factual accuracy, originality, rights, platform format, and affiliate disclosure.
5. CEO reviews and approves the draft.
6. Publish manually or through a verified official integration; never simulate engagement.
7. Record measured results and distinguish estimates from confirmed transactions/commissions.
8. Review results on a regular schedule and decide whether to repeat, revise, or stop.

**Pilot exit gate:** the workflow can be repeated reliably; every published item has a record; sources and rights are traceable; disclosures are present where required; costs and results are measurable; and the CEO has enough verified data to decide whether the pilot should continue. A sale or commission is not guaranteed and is not required to call the workflow technically tested.

## 4. Master development phases — in order

### Phase A — Protect, inspect, and establish the baseline
**Priority:** Immediate  
**Funding:** minimal, CEO-approved operational effort  
**Status:** baseline documentation exists; verify the actual machine/runtime before implementation.

Tasks:
- Keep an untouched copy of the supplied Phase D v8 package.
- Record file hashes and the exact source package/version.
- Back up user data, settings, API credentials, Bazaar orders, and audit logs without exposing secrets in reports.
- Confirm how the existing app starts, what is already working, and what tests pass.
- Create a change log and a repeatable rollback procedure.

**Exit gate:** clean baseline copy, verified startup/test instructions, protected-file list, and recoverable backup.

### Phase B — Run the tiny business pilot
**Priority:** Highest  
**Funding:** only the small amount explicitly approved by CEO.

Tasks:
- Select one channel and one narrow content/product category.
- Confirm account access and relevant affiliate/platform rules.
- Prepare a simple content calendar and product/topic shortlist.
- Use AI for research and drafting; keep human approval before publication.
- Track published items, links, views, clicks, reported conversions, confirmed commissions, costs, and time spent.
- Review quality and results before increasing output.

**Exit gate:** documented repeatable workflow, compliance checklist, measurable costs/results, and an evidence-based continue/change/stop decision.

### Phase C — Build a minimum viable AI business dashboard
**Priority:** High, after the pilot workflow is understood.

Tasks:
- Show work queue, draft status, review status, publish log, and agent/task status.
- Show source references and last-checked timestamps.
- Separate estimated, pending, confirmed, reversed, and paid commission values where source data supports them.
- Show operating costs and simple net-result reporting.
- Add CEO pause controls for agents and publishing queues.
- Keep secrets server-side; never expose credentials in browser code.

**Exit gate:** dashboard data reconciles with source records; access controls and pause controls are tested; no unverified estimate is presented as cash received.

### Phase D — Add safe, limited automation
**Priority:** High, only after manual workflow is stable.

Tasks:
- Automate one low-risk repeatable task at a time (for example, draft generation or reporting).
- Add idempotency, bounded retries, failure logs, rate limits, and task ownership.
- Implement publishing outcomes: AUTO-PUBLISH, HOLD FOR REVIEW, BLOCK.
- Begin in draft/sandbox mode. Enable auto-publishing only for approved content types and authorized integrations after test posts and permission checks.
- Add a global publishing kill switch and audit trail.

**Exit gate:** negative tests pass; duplicate publishing is prevented; uncertain/high-risk content is held; prohibited content is blocked; CEO can pause the system.

### Phase E — Financial integrity and reinvestment readiness
**Priority:** Critical before significant project expansion.

Tasks:
- Reconcile source-reported transactions and commissions.
- Separate pending estimates, confirmed amounts, reversals, paid amounts, fees, refunds, taxes/obligations, and operating expenses.
- Define CEO-controlled reserve requirements and available-funds calculation.
- Keep operating funds, emergency reserves, development fund, and player reward budget distinct.
- Produce an AI recommendation report with cost, expected benefit, risk, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and rollback plan.

**Available development capacity must be calculated from verified funds after applicable costs and obligations and CEO-defined reserves.** No fixed percentage or threshold is preselected.

**Exit gate:** financial reports reconcile; reserve and fund rules are configured by CEO; AI recommendations cannot trigger spending; approval records are auditable.

### Phase F — Small improvements to the existing company office
**Priority:** Medium; after business operations are reliable and a specific upgrade has a clear benefit.

Choose only one small upgrade per approved batch, for example:
- improve visibility of agent/task status;
- add failed/queued/idle counters;
- improve backup/health reporting;
- add a department activity summary;
- improve accessibility/performance controls.

Do not start a large office redesign merely because it appears in the 100-item layout list. Select upgrades based on operational value, affordability, and risk.

**Exit gate:** upgrade is useful, tested, reversible, and did not regress the protected office.

### Phase G — Shared player foundation
**Priority:** Later; only after CEO-approved budget and business-first gate.

Tasks in small batches:
1. Account/session and roles.
2. Player profile and avatar data.
3. Secure persistence and moderation controls.
4. Basic levels/XP and server-authoritative records.
5. One job/profession and a small quest board.

Do not add cash-out, cash conversion, real-money trading, or paid random loot.

**Exit gate:** account security, data isolation, server-side validation, abuse controls, and backup/restore tests pass.

### Phase H — Multiplayer office and social layer
**Priority:** Later; dependent on shared player foundation and a separately approved budget.

Tasks:
- Install a maintained real-time transport and generate a lockfile in a network-enabled environment.
- Add guest/registered session rules, connection limits, heartbeat, and reconnect handling.
- Add remote avatars, presence, shared chat, moderation, and two-browser tests.
- Keep guests away from private company rooms, finance, publishing, and secrets.

**Exit gate:** two or more real browser clients see synchronized state; moderation and reconnect tests pass; measured capacity is documented without unsupported promises.

### Phase I — Small seamless world prototype
**Priority:** Later; do not build the whole world at once.

Tasks:
- Prototype one connected route from Company Campus to Central Plaza to a tiny training area.
- Keep world shell and district code isolated behind feature flags.
- Test movement, transitions, loading, reconnect, and return to office.
- Keep the office usable if Adventure services are disabled.

**Exit gate:** continuous travel works in the tested prototype; loading/performance and rollback are verified.

### Phase J — First Adventure vertical slice
**Priority:** Later; separately funded and approved.

Smallest playable scope from the existing design:
- one starter zone;
- one playable character/class;
- third-person movement and camera;
- basic attack, dodge, and one skill;
- three fictional insect enemies: Giant Ant Scout, Mutant Mosquito, Armored Cockroach;
- one short quest;
- one small boss only after basic combat is stable.

Server validates movement, collisions, attack range, cooldowns, enemy health, XP, inventory, drops, and quest completion.

**Exit gate:** tested playable slice with server authority, abuse tests, and two-client shared encounter validation before expanding content.

### Phase K — Progression, crafting, marketplace, and rewards
**Priority:** Later; each system gets its own budget and release gate.

- Add jobs, quests, achievements, crafting, inventory, and game-only progression incrementally.
- Keep Game Coins, XP, items, Company Reward Credits, affiliate commissions, and Bazaar balances separate.
- Gameplay quests grant gameplay rewards by default.
- Company-funded rewards require a separate CEO-approved budget funded only by confirmed, reconciled company revenue.
- Hybrid rewards may include a shared company-funded pool and individual verified-contribution game rewards; neither is cash or withdrawable.
- Verify affiliate-program/platform terms before player-specific referrals or incentives. Disable unsupported paths.
- Do not disguise unpaid commercial work as gameplay.

**Exit gate:** transactionality, idempotency, caps, fraud review, audit logs, clear player rules, and CEO pause/approval controls pass tests.

### Phase L — Expansion and controlled beta
**Priority:** Last, only when the earlier systems are stable and affordable.

- Add zones, parties, events, dungeons, bosses, and guilds in separate milestones.
- Run load tests and monitor latency, memory, CPU, errors, disconnects, and queue backlog.
- Start with a limited closed beta at a concurrency level supported by measured tests.
- Expand infrastructure only after the CEO approves the operating cost and funding source.

**Exit gate:** support, moderation, security, backups, restore drills, performance, and incident response are ready for the intended pilot scale.

## 5. How the AI Director recommends an upgrade

Every recommendation must contain:
- Problem or opportunity being addressed.
- Evidence and source of the evidence.
- Why it should be prioritized now.
- Estimated one-time and recurring cost, with uncertainty clearly stated.
- Expected business or product benefit (not guaranteed income).
- Dependencies and risks.
- Smallest viable scope.
- Acceptance tests and success criteria.
- Rollback plan.
- Proposed funding source and maximum requested budget.

The recommendation is not an approval. Work that spends money or changes production requires an explicit CEO approval record.

## 6. CEO approval record template

For every funded batch, record:

| Field | Required value |
|---|---|
| Batch ID | Unique identifier |
| Selected milestone | Exact phase/task being approved |
| Scope | Included and excluded work |
| Funding source | Approved company fund/operating budget |
| Maximum budget | CEO-set cap; no invented amount |
| Recurring cost | Monthly/annual estimate if applicable |
| Success criteria | Measurable acceptance checks |
| Rollback point | Known-good version and recovery steps |
| Approval | CEO identity and timestamp |
| Actual result | Cost, test results, issues, decision |

If any field is missing, the batch remains **NOT APPROVED**.

## 7. Simple financial control model

Use separate records for:
- Operating expenses and payable obligations.
- Tax/refund/chargeback/platform-fee provisions as applicable.
- CEO-defined reserves.
- Development fund.
- Separate player reward budget.
- Affiliate commission ledger.
- Bazaar order and money records.
- Game economy ledgers (XP, Game Coins, items, Company Reward Credits).

Do not calculate development capacity from gross sales, projected commissions, pending orders, or unvalidated affiliate estimates.

A safe conceptual calculation is:

**Potential available development funds = confirmed and reconciled company funds − applicable operating costs and obligations − CEO-defined reserves − already committed amounts.**

The result is only a recommendation input. The CEO still approves the actual batch and cap.

## 9. What we should do first — the next practical action

Do not begin by building more MMORPG features. Begin by confirming the smallest business pilot:

1. Pick the first channel where the CEO already has or can lawfully obtain access.
2. Pick one narrow content/product category.
3. Confirm affiliate eligibility, permitted traffic sources, disclosure requirements, and platform publishing rules.
4. Set a small maximum pilot budget (or zero-cost/manual mode) and time limit; CEO approves it.
5. Prepare the first small batch of drafts, review them, and publish only when approved.
6. Track actual results for a defined review period.
7. Decide to stop, adjust, or repeat based on evidence.

Only after the workflow and financial records are reliable should the company fund the next upgrade.
